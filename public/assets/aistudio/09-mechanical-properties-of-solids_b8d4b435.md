## Elasticity, Stress & Strain

1. Interatomic forces act like springs. Elasticity is the property of regaining shape when the deforming force is removed. Perfectly plastic bodies don't regain it.
2. Stress = internal restoring force per unit area. Types: longitudinal (tensile/compressive), shearing, bulk (volume).

$$\text{stress}=\frac FA\ \ (\text{N m}^{-2})$$

3. Strain (dimensionless): longitudinal $\Delta l/l$, shearing $\theta\approx x/l$, volume $\Delta V/V$.

## Hooke's Law & Elastic Moduli

4. Hooke's law: within the elastic limit, stress ∝ strain. The constant is the modulus of elasticity.

$$Y=\frac{F/A}{\Delta l/l}\ \Rightarrow\ \Delta l=\frac{Fl}{AY}$$

$$B=-\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V/V},\qquad \eta=\frac{F/A}{\theta}$$

(For a wire of length $l$ loaded by $Mg$: $Y=\dfrac{Mg/A}{\Delta l/l}$.)

5. Compressibility $=1/B$. For gases: isothermal $B=P$, adiabatic $B=\gamma P$. Poisson's ratio $\sigma=-\dfrac{\Delta d/d}{\Delta l/l}$ (theory: $-1$ to $0.5$).

$$Y=2\eta(1+\sigma)=3B(1-2\sigma)$$

6. Stress–strain curve: proportional limit → elastic limit → yield point → plastic region → ultimate strength → fracture. Ductile materials have a long plastic region, brittle ones break soon after the elastic limit. Elastomers (rubber) have no linear region. The slope of the linear part = $Y$.
7. Breaking stress depends only on the material. Max load = breaking stress × area.
8. Wire hanging under its own weight: $\Delta l=\dfrac{Mgl}{2AY}$ (half of a load $Mg$ at the end). Thermal stress in a clamped rod heated by $\Delta T$: $Y\alpha\Delta T$.

> $Y$, $B$ and $\eta$ are material constants. Doubling the length or radius changes $\Delta l$, not $Y$. $Y$ decreases with temperature.

> Elongation questions (series wires, two wires with blocks, own weight, ratio when $L$ and $d$ change) are the most asked elasticity questions. Breaking stress with a lift accelerating, and bulk modulus with depth ($\Delta V/V=\rho gh/B$) are also asked.

## Elastic Potential Energy & Measuring Y

9. Energy stored in a stretched wire (area under the $F$–$\Delta l$ graph):

$$U=\tfrac12F\Delta l=\tfrac12\,\text{stress}\times\text{strain}\times\text{volume}$$

$$u=\frac UV=\tfrac12Y(\text{strain})^2=\frac{(\text{stress})^2}{2Y}$$

10. Searle's method (Experimental Skill): $Y=\dfrac{Mgl}{\pi r^2\Delta l}$. Plot load vs extension, take the slope, and use a reference wire to cancel temperature and sag effects.

**Tricky points**
- A soap bubble has two surfaces: $4T/R$ and $2T\Delta A$.
- Stress at a point in a heavy wire = (load + weight of the part below)/A.
- Isothermal bulk modulus of a gas = $P$.

**Mistakes to avoid**
- Forgetting 1 poise = 0.1 Pa s.
- Using diameter instead of radius in Stokes' law.
