> Fluids at rest (pressure, Pascal, Archimedes) and fluids in motion (continuity, Bernoulli). Surface tension and viscosity are covered below.

## Pressure in a Fluid & Pascal's Law

1. A fluid at rest exerts force only perpendicular to any surface. Pressure = normal force per unit area (a scalar; 1 Pa = 1 N m⁻², 1 atm = $1.013\times10^5$ Pa).

$$P=\frac{dF}{dA}$$

2. Pressure increases with depth. Points at the same level in the same continuous liquid have the same pressure, whatever the shape of the vessel.

$$P=P_0+\rho gh$$

3. Pascal's law: a pressure change applied to an enclosed liquid is transmitted undiminished to every point. Hydraulic lift / press:

$$\frac{F_1}{A_1}=\frac{F_2}{A_2}\ \Rightarrow\ F_2=F_1\frac{A_2}{A_1}$$

4. In a container accelerating up with $a$: $P=P_0+\rho(g+a)h$. Accelerating horizontally: the free surface tilts, $\tan\theta=a/g$.

## Atmospheric Pressure & Barometer

5. A mercury barometer holds a column of height $h$ where $P_0=\rho_{Hg}gh$ (76 cm at 1 atm). Manometer: pressure difference $=\rho gh$ (gauge pressure). Absolute pressure = atmospheric + gauge.

## Archimedes' Principle & Buoyancy

6. A body (wholly or partly) immersed in a fluid feels an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced, acting at the centre of buoyancy.

$$F_B=\rho_{fluid}V_{sub}\,g$$

7. Floating: weight = buoyant force ⇒ fraction submerged $=\rho_{body}/\rho_{liquid}$. Apparent weight in a liquid $=mg-F_B$. Relative density = weight in air / loss of weight in water.

$$\frac{V_{sub}}{V}=\frac{\rho_{body}}{\rho_{liquid}}$$

8. Extra load $m$ on a floating body sinks it further by a volume $m/\rho_{liquid}$.

> Floating cube + coin: the extra submerged depth × area × $\rho$ gives the coin's mass (2026). Hydraulic lift and flotation are also asked.

## Fluid Flow & Equation of Continuity

9. Steady (streamline) flow: the velocity at a point doesn't change with time, and streamlines never cross. Turbulent flow is irregular (above the critical velocity). Ideal fluid: incompressible, non-viscous, irrotational. Streamlines crowd where the flow is faster.
10. Continuity (mass conservation, incompressible fluid):

$$A_1v_1=A_2v_2=\text{volume flow rate}$$

Narrow section: faster flow ($v_2>v_1$).

> A spray gun with $n$ holes: $Av=nav'$. Check cm² and mm² conversions.

## Bernoulli's Equation & Applications

11. Along a streamline of an ideal fluid (energy conservation per unit volume):

$$P+\tfrac12\rho v^2+\rho gh=\text{constant}$$

12. Torricelli's theorem: speed of efflux from a hole at depth $h$ below the free surface:

$$v=\sqrt{2gh}$$

$$\text{range on ground}=2\sqrt{h(H-h)}\quad(\text{max }H\text{ at }h=H/2)$$

13. Venturimeter: the pressure difference across the throat gives the flow rate $v_1=A_2\sqrt{\dfrac{2\Delta P}{\rho(A_1^2-A_2^2)}}$. Other uses: the Pitot tube, the lift on an aeroplane wing, an atomiser, a roof blown off in a storm, the Magnus effect. (Throat: higher speed, lower pressure.)

> Bernoulli + continuity (same level: $\Delta P=\tfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)$) and Torricelli + projectile range appear 3–5 times a year.

## Surface Tension & Excess Pressure

14. Surface tension $T$: force per unit length acting along the surface, perpendicular to an imaginary line on it. It equals the surface energy per unit area.

$$T=\frac Fl=\frac{\Delta W}{\Delta A}\quad(\text{N m}^{-1}=\text{J m}^{-2})$$

A film has two surfaces: $F=2Tl$.

15. Soap film / bubble has two surfaces: the work to increase the film area by $\Delta A$ is $2T\Delta A$.

$$W_{bubble}=2T\cdot4\pi\left(r_2^2-r_1^2\right)=8\pi T\left(r_2^2-r_1^2\right)$$

16. Excess pressure inside a curved surface:

$$\text{drop: }\Delta P=\frac{2T}{R},\qquad \text{bubble (2 surfaces): }\Delta P=\frac{4T}{R}$$

17. Splitting a drop of radius $R$ into $n$ equal droplets: $r=R/n^{1/3}$. The energy needed equals the increase in surface area × $T$. Merging releases the same energy (and heats the drop).

$$\Delta E=4\pi R^2T\left(n^{1/3}-1\right)$$

> Surface energy (splitting/merging drops, blowing bubbles) is a top fluid topic: 5 of the 8 April-2026 shifts you uploaded had one. Remember the factor 2 for bubbles.

## Contact Angle & Capillary Rise

18. Contact angle $\theta$ (measured inside the liquid): acute → the liquid wets the solid and rises (water–glass ≈ 0°). Obtuse → it doesn't wet and falls (mercury–glass ≈ 135°). It depends on the solid–liquid pair, not on the tube radius.
19. Capillary rise (Jurin's law):

$$h=\frac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g}\qquad(hr=\text{constant})$$

20. In a tube shorter than $h$ the liquid does not overflow: the meniscus radius adjusts so that $R'h'=Rh$. Detergents lower $T$, so the rise is smaller (Experimental Skill).

> Capillary questions: change in rise with radius or $T$, mercury depression, A-R on why oil spreads (contact angle).

## Viscosity, Stokes' Law, Terminal Velocity

21. Viscosity: internal friction between fluid layers moving relative to each other. Newton's law:

$$F=-\eta A\frac{dv}{dx}\quad(\eta\text{ in Pa s};\ 1\text{ poise}=0.1\text{ Pa s})$$

22. Poiseuille's equation (flow through a narrow tube of radius $r$, length $l$). The velocity profile is parabolic, fastest on the axis.

$$Q=\frac{\pi Pr^4}{8\eta l}$$

23. Stokes' law: viscous drag on a sphere moving slowly: $F=6\pi\eta rv$. At terminal speed: $mg=B+6\pi\eta rv_t$.
24. Terminal velocity: weight = buoyancy + drag.

$$v_t=\frac{2r^2(\rho-\sigma)g}{9\eta}$$

25. $v_t\propto r^2$. An air bubble rising through a liquid uses $(\rho_{liquid}-0)$. Drops merging into one big drop: $v_t$ increases by $n^{2/3}$.
26. Critical velocity & Reynolds number: flow is streamline when $Re=\dfrac{\rho vD}{\eta}$ is small (< ~2000) and turbulent when large (> ~3000).

> Viscosity / terminal velocity is the most asked fluid topic: drop broken into 64 droplets ($v_t$ falls by 16), bubble rising, viscosity from steady fall.
