# Kinetic Theory

- Class: 11 Physics
- Chapter no.: 13
- Weightage: High

> The pressure and temperature of a gas come from the random motion of its molecules.

## Assumptions of Kinetic Theory

- A gas is a very large number of identical molecules. Their size is negligible compared with the separation.
- They move randomly. There are no forces except during collisions, which are perfectly elastic and of negligible duration.
- Gravity is ignored, and Newton's laws apply.

## Pressure, RMS Speed, Kinetic Interpretation of Temperature

1. Pressure from momentum transfer at the walls (each wall hit changes momentum by $2mv_x$):

$$p=\frac13\rho\overline{v^2}=\frac13\frac{mN}{V}v_{rms}^2=\frac23\frac{E_{trans}}{V}$$

2. Comparing with $pV=nRT$, the average translational KE depends only on $T$:

$$\tfrac12m\overline{v^2}=\tfrac32k_BT$$

$$v_{rms}=\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}=\sqrt{\frac{3k_BT}{m}}=\sqrt{\frac{3p}{\rho}}$$

3. Other speeds: $v_{av}=\sqrt{\dfrac{8RT}{\pi M}}$, $v_{mp}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2RT}{M}}$. Order: $v_{rms}>v_{av}>v_{mp}$ (ratio $\sqrt3:\sqrt{8/\pi}:\sqrt2$).

> Same average KE ⇒ same $T$ (true). Same $T$ ⇒ same $v_{rms}$ for H₂ and O₂ (false, since $v\propto1/\sqrt M$). The temperature at which one gas's $v_{rms}$ equals another's: $T_1/M_1=T_2/M_2$.

## Deductions & Ideal Gas Equation

4. Kinetic theory gives Boyle's, Charles', Avogadro's and Graham's laws ($v\propto1/\sqrt\rho$) and Dalton's law of partial pressures $p=p_1+p_2+\ldots$.
5. Mean free path (average distance between collisions) for molecules of diameter $d$ and number density $n$:

$$\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi d^2n}=\frac{k_BT}{\sqrt2\,\pi d^2p}$$

> Mean free path $\propto1/d^2$ and $\propto T/p$. Collision frequency $=v_{av}/\lambda$.

## Maxwell Distribution, State, Brownian Motion

6. Maxwell's speed distribution: few molecules are very slow or very fast. At higher $T$ the peak shifts right and the curve flattens. The area (total number) stays the same.
7. The thermodynamic state is fixed by $p$, $V$, $T$. Brownian motion (random motion of suspended particles) is direct evidence of molecular motion. It increases with $T$ and decreases with particle size and viscosity.

## Vapour, Humidity, Phase Diagram

8. Saturated vapour pressure (SVP) depends only on temperature, not on volume. Boiling: SVP = external pressure, so water boils at a lower temperature at high altitude.
9. Relative humidity = (vapour pressure present) / (SVP at the same temperature). Dew point: the temperature at which the present vapour becomes saturated.

$$RH=\frac{\text{SVP at dew point}}{\text{SVP at air temperature}}$$

10. Phase diagram: the triple point (water: 273.16 K, 4.58 mm Hg) is where solid, liquid and vapour coexist. The critical point is the temperature above which a gas can't be liquefied by pressure alone. (Water: the fusion line leans back.)

## Equipartition & Mixtures

11. Each degree of freedom carries $\tfrac12k_BT$ per molecule. Internal energy of $n$ moles:

$$U=\frac f2nRT\quad(f=3\text{ mono},\ 5\text{ dia},\ 6\text{ non-linear poly};\ +2\text{ per vibrational mode})$$

$$\gamma=1+\frac2f$$

12. Mixing gases in an insulated vessel keeps $U$ constant:

$$T_{mix}=\frac{n_1f_1T_1+n_2f_2T_2}{n_1f_1+n_2f_2}$$

$$\frac{n_1+n_2}{\gamma_{mix}-1}=\frac{n_1}{\gamma_1-1}+\frac{n_2}{\gamma_2-1}$$

> Gas mixtures (frequently asked): the mixture temperature, $\gamma$ or $C_v$ weighted by moles (not mass), and the moles of each gas from the total mass.

**Tricky points**
- The average KE depends only on $T$, not on the type of gas.
- Doubling $T$ gives $\sqrt2$ times $v_{rms}$.

**Mistakes to avoid**
- Using $T$ in °C, or $M$ in g/mol inside SI formulas.
