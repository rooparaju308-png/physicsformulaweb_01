# Work Energy and Power

- Class: 11 Physics
- Chapter no.: 6

> The work–energy theorem replaces force–time analysis with force–displacement analysis. Use it whenever speed at a position is asked.

## Kinetic Energy & Work–Energy Theorem

1. Kinetic energy $K=\tfrac12mv^2$ (scalar, ≥ 0). Work by a constant force: $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=Fd\cos\theta$. Positive if $\theta<90^\circ$, zero if ⊥, negative if $\theta>90^\circ$.

$$K=\tfrac12mv^2=\frac{p^2}{2m},\qquad W=Fd\cos\theta$$

2. Work–energy theorem: the work done by all forces (conservative, non-conservative, pseudo) equals the change in KE.

$$W_{total}=\Delta K=\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2$$

## Calculation of Work; Variable Forces

3. Variable force: work $=\int\vec F\cdot d\vec r$ = area under the $F$–$x$ graph.

$$W=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}F(x)\,dx$$

4. Spring force $F=-kx$. Work done by the spring from $x_1$ to $x_2$:

$$W_{spring}=\tfrac12k\left(x_1^2-x_2^2\right)$$

5. Work done by gravity is $mg\times$(fall in height), whatever the path. Work done by a normal force or tension can be non-zero when the surface or support moves.

> Given $v=f(x)$, the work done between two points is just $\Delta K$. You don't need the force (2026 shift). Penetration/resistive-force questions: $mg(h+d)=F_{av}d$ for a ball falling into sand.

## Potential Energy, Conservative Forces

6. A force is conservative if its work is independent of path (equivalently, zero work around any closed path): gravity, spring, electrostatic. Friction and air drag are non-conservative.
7. Potential energy is defined only for conservative forces:

$$\Delta U=U_f-U_i=-W_{conservative},\qquad F=-\frac{dU}{dx},\quad \vec F=-\nabla U$$

8. Equilibrium where $dU/dx=0$. Stable: $U$ is minimum ($d^2U/dx^2>0$), restoring. Unstable: $U$ is maximum. Neutral: $U$ is constant.
9. Conservation of mechanical energy: if only conservative forces do work, $K+U$ = constant. With non-conservative forces:

$$W_{nc}=\Delta(K+U)$$

## Gravitational & Spring PE, Forms of Energy, Power

10. Near the earth $U=mgh$. A spring stretched or compressed by $x$ stores $U=\tfrac12kx^2$.

$$U_g=mgh,\qquad U_{spring}=\tfrac12kx^2$$

($F=-kx=-dU/dx$.)

11. Power: rate of doing work. Unit: watt (1 hp = 746 W). Efficiency = output power / input power.

$$P=\frac{dW}{dt}=\vec F\cdot\vec v,\qquad P_{av}=\frac Wt$$

12. Constant power engine starting from rest: $Pt=\tfrac12mv^2$ ⇒ $v\propto t^{1/2}$, $x\propto t^{3/2}$. For uniform acceleration from rest, instantaneous power = 2 × average power.
13. Energy has many forms (mechanical, thermal, electrical, chemical, nuclear, mass-energy $E=mc^2$). The total energy of an isolated system is always conserved.

> Power with a time-dependent force: find $v(t)$ from $\int F\,dt/m$, then $P=\vec F\cdot\vec v$. Also common: $p=\sqrt{2mK}$ ratio questions ("KE increased by 300% → momentum doubles").

## Collisions

14. In any collision, momentum is conserved (the impulsive forces are internal). KE is conserved only in elastic collisions.
15. Coefficient of restitution (along the line of impact): $e=1$ elastic, $0<e<1$ inelastic, $e=0$ perfectly inelastic (bodies stick).

$$e=\frac{\text{velocity of separation}}{\text{velocity of approach}}=\frac{v_2-v_1}{u_1-u_2}$$

16. 1-D elastic collision:

$$v_1=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1+\frac{2m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_2,\qquad v_2=\frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_1+\frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_2$$

- Equal masses exchange velocities.
- Heavy body hits a light one at rest: the light one moves off with ≈ $2u$.
- Light body hits a heavy one at rest: it rebounds with ≈ the same speed.
- Fraction of KE transferred to $m_2$ (at rest): $\dfrac{4m_1m_2}{(m_1+m_2)^2}$.

17. Perfectly inelastic: common velocity and KE loss:

$$v=\frac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad \Delta K=\frac{m_1m_2}{2(m_1+m_2)}(u_1-u_2)^2$$

18. Ballistic pendulum: a bullet embeds in the block (momentum conserved), then the block rises (energy conserved):

$$h=\frac1{2g}\left(\frac{mv}{m+M}\right)^2$$

19. 2-D elastic collision: conserve momentum along both axes. Equal masses with one at rest move off at 90° to each other.
20. Ball dropped from height $h$ with restitution $e$: rebound height $e^2h$, after $n$ bounces $e^{2n}h$ (heights $h,\ e^2h,\ e^4h,\ \ldots$).

> A spring between two blocks is compressed most when they share a common velocity, not when one is at rest.

## Impulse & Impulsive Force

21. Impulse = change in momentum = area under the $F$–$t$ graph. Impulsive forces are large forces acting for a very short time (bat on ball). During such short times, finite forces like gravity can be ignored.

$$\vec J=\int\vec F\,dt=\Delta\vec p$$

22. Ball hitting a wall normally and rebounding with the same speed: $\Delta p=2mv$. Average force $=2mv/\Delta t$. Machine gun: $F=nmv$ (n bullets per second).

> Impulse is asked often: $F$–$t$ graph area, wall rebound ($2mv$), machine-gun recoil ($F=nmv$).
