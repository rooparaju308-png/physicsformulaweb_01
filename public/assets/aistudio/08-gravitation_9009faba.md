# Gravitation

- Class: 11 Physics
- Chapter no.: 8
- Weightage: High

> Newton's law of gravitation is universal. With field, potential and energy it explains $g$, satellites, escape and Kepler's laws.

## Law of Gravitation & G

1. Every particle attracts every other particle with a force along the line joining them:

$$F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2},\qquad G=6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}$$

2. Gravitational forces obey superposition. A uniform spherical shell or sphere attracts an external particle as if all its mass were at its centre. $G$ was measured by Cavendish (torsion balance).

(Force vs $r/R_E$: hollow shell gives 0 inside; solid sphere gives $\propto r$ inside.)

## Gravitational PE & Potential

3. PE of two particles (zero at infinity), and potential = PE per unit mass:

$$U=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r},\qquad V=-\frac{GM}{r}$$

| Distribution | Potential $V$ |
|---|---|
| Point mass / outside sphere or shell | $-GM/r$ |
| Ring on axis (distance $x$) | $-\dfrac{GM}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}$ |
| Inside spherical shell | $-GM/a$ (constant) |
| Inside uniform solid sphere | $-\dfrac{GM}{2a^3}(3a^2-r^2)$; centre $=-\dfrac{3GM}{2a}$ |

(For a solid sphere, $V$ at the centre is $-1.5\,GM/R$.)

4. For several masses add the PE of every pair. Work done by an external agent = change in PE.

## Gravitational Field

5. Field = force per unit mass. Relation with potential:

$$\vec E=\frac{\vec F}{m},\qquad E=-\frac{dV}{dr}$$

| Distribution | Field $E$ |
|---|---|
| Point mass / outside sphere | $GM/r^2$ |
| Ring on axis | $\dfrac{GMx}{(a^2+x^2)^{3/2}}$ (max at $x=a/\sqrt2$) |
| Inside spherical shell | 0 |
| Inside uniform solid sphere | $\dfrac{GMr}{a^3}$ ($\propto r$) |

## Variation of g

6. At the surface $g=GM/R^2$. It varies with height, depth and latitude:

$$g_h=\frac{g}{(1+h/R)^2}\approx g\left(1-\frac{2h}{R}\right)\ (h\ll R)$$

$$g_d=g\left(1-\frac dR\right),\qquad g'=g-\omega^2R\cos^2\lambda$$

($g\propto r$ inside, $\propto1/r^2$ outside.)

7. $g$ is zero at the centre and maximum at the surface. $g$ at height $R$ is $g/4$, and at height $2R$ it is $g/9$.

> $g$ changes with height or depth (very frequently asked): "% change from depth $d$ to height $h$", "height where $g$ becomes $g/n$", "pendulum clock at altitude runs slow".

## Planets, Satellites & Kepler's Laws

8. Orbital speed and period of a satellite at distance $r$ from the centre (it does not depend on the satellite's mass). Gravity supplies $mv^2/r$; $r=R+h$.

$$v_o=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}$$

9. Kepler's laws: (1) orbits are ellipses with the sun at one focus; (2) a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times (conservation of angular momentum); (3) $T^2\propto a^3$.

$$\frac{dA}{dt}=\frac{L}{2m}=\text{const},\qquad T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$$

10. Energy of a satellite: KE $=\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$, PE $=-\dfrac{GMm}{r}$, total $E=-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$ (bound). Binding energy $=+\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$.

$$E=-K=\tfrac12U$$

11. Geostationary satellite: period 24 h, equatorial orbit at height about 36000 km. Weightlessness in a satellite: everything inside is in free fall with the same acceleration, so normal forces are zero.

> Kepler's 3rd law around different central masses: $T^2\propto r^3/M$. A satellite skimming the surface has $T=2\pi\sqrt{R/g}=\sqrt{3\pi/G\rho}$, which depends only on density (2026 statement question).

## Escape Velocity, Binding Energy, Black Holes

12. Escape velocity: the minimum speed to reach infinity ($KE+PE\ge0$). It is independent of the mass of the body and the direction of projection.

$$v_e=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}=\sqrt{2gR}\approx11.2\ \text{km/s},\qquad v_e=\sqrt2\,v_o\ \text{(near surface)}$$

13. A body falling from infinity reaches the earth with $v_e$ and KE $=mgR$. Energy needed to lift a body to height $h$: $\Delta U=\dfrac{mgh}{1+h/R}$ (to height $nR$: $\dfrac{n}{n+1}mgR$).
14. Gravitational binding energy of a uniform sphere: $\dfrac{3GM^2}{5R}$. A black hole has $v_e>c$ (Schwarzschild radius $2GM/c^2$).
15. Inertial mass ($F=ma$) and gravitational mass ($F=GMm/r^2$) are found experimentally to be equal (the basis of general relativity).

> Escape/energy questions are the most asked group: energy to send a body to height $2R$ or to infinity, $v_e\propto\sqrt{M/R}$ for another planet, satellite energy when the orbit changes.
