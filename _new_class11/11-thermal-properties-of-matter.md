# Thermal Properties of Matter

- Class: 11 Physics
- Chapter no.: 11
- Weightage: Medium

> Temperature is what two bodies have equal when they are in thermal equilibrium. This chapter covers how to measure it and how matter expands with it.

## Hot & Cold Bodies; Zeroth Law

1. Heat flows from the hotter to the colder body until thermal equilibrium. Zeroth law: if A and B are each in equilibrium with C, then A and B are in equilibrium with each other. This defines temperature.

> Statement questions: "the zeroth law gives the concept of temperature, the first law gives the concept of internal energy" (both true).

## Temperature Scales & Thermometers

2. A scale uses a thermometric property (length of a liquid column, resistance, gas pressure) that varies linearly with temperature between two fixed points.

$$t=\frac{X_t-X_0}{X_{100}-X_0}\times100^\circ$$

3. Conversions (for temperature differences: $\Delta F=\tfrac95\Delta C$, $\Delta K=\Delta C$):

$$\frac{C-0}{100}=\frac{F-32}{180}=\frac{K-273.15}{100}$$

(Water: 0 °C = 32 °F = 273 K; 100 °C = 212 °F = 373 K.)

4. Platinum resistance thermometer: $R_t=R_0(1+\alpha t)$, read with a Wheatstone bridge. Constant-volume gas thermometer: $T=273.16\,\dfrac{p}{p_{tr}}$ K (triple point of water = 273.16 K). The ideal-gas scale takes the limit as the gas pressure → 0. It agrees with the absolute (Kelvin) scale.
5. $-40^\circ$C $=-40^\circ$F. Callendar's compensated thermometer cancels the effect of the connecting wires.

> Scale-conversion questions: two arbitrary scales with different fixed points (use linear interpolation), and differences on °F vs °C.

## Ideal Gas Equation

6. Boyle's law ($pV$ = const at fixed $T$) and Charles' law ($V\propto T$ at fixed $p$) combine into:

$$pV=nRT=Nk_BT$$

$$R=8.314\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1},\qquad k_B=\frac{R}{N_A}=1.38\times10^{-23}\ \text{J K}^{-1}$$

(On a $p$–$V$ graph the isotherm for $T_2>T_1$ lies farther from the origin.)

## Adiabatic/Diathermic Walls & Thermal Expansion

7. Adiabatic walls don't let heat through. Diathermic walls do.
8. Thermal expansion (coefficients $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$):

$$\Delta l=l\alpha\Delta T,\qquad \Delta A=A\beta\Delta T,\qquad \Delta V=V\gamma\Delta T$$

$$\beta=2\alpha,\qquad \gamma=3\alpha$$

9. A hole in a plate or a cavity expands exactly as if it were filled with the same material.
10. Liquids: real expansion = apparent expansion + expansion of the container: $\gamma_{real}=\gamma_{app}+\gamma_{vessel}$. Density falls with temperature: $\rho_T=\dfrac{\rho_0}{1+\gamma\Delta T}$.
11. Anomalous expansion of water: between 0 and 4 °C water contracts on heating. Its density is maximum at 4 °C, which is why lakes freeze from the top.
12. Applications: bimetallic strip (bends towards the metal with lower $\alpha$ when heated), pendulum clock (loses $\tfrac12\alpha\Delta T$ fraction of time when heated), measuring-scale errors, gaps in railway tracks. Thermal stress in a clamped rod: $Y\alpha\Delta T$.
13. Gas at constant pressure: $\gamma=1/T$. For a process $PT^n$ = const, $V\propto T^{n+1}$, so $\gamma=(n+1)/T$.

> Expansion questions: linear expansion over two temperature intervals, a hole in a sheet, the volume-expansion coefficient of a gas in a given process ($PT^2$ = const ⇒ $3/T$).

## Calorimetry

Heat is energy in transit because of a temperature difference. Calorimetry is energy bookkeeping: heat lost = heat gained.

### Heat as Energy, Units, Principle of Calorimetry

14. Unit: joule. 1 calorie = 4.186 J (the heat to raise 1 g of water from 14.5 to 15.5 °C).
15. Principle of calorimetry: in an isolated system, heat lost by the hot bodies = heat gained by the cold bodies.

$$\sum\text{heat lost}=\sum\text{heat gained}$$

### Specific Heat & Heat Capacity

16. Specific heat capacity $s$, molar heat capacity $C$, heat capacity (thermal capacity) $ms$, water equivalent $W=ms/s_{water}$.

$$Q=ms\Delta T,\qquad C=\frac1n\frac{dQ}{dT}$$

17. Water: $s=4200$ J kg⁻¹K⁻¹ (1 cal g⁻¹°C⁻¹). Ice ≈ 2100 J kg⁻¹K⁻¹. Specific heat is measured by the method of mixtures (Experimental Skill).

### Latent Heat

18. During a phase change the temperature stays constant. Heat is used to change the state:

$$Q=mL$$

$$L_{fusion,ice}=336\ \text{kJ/kg}\ (80\text{ cal/g}),\qquad L_{vap,water}=2256\ \text{kJ/kg}\ (540\text{ cal/g})$$

19. Slopes of the heating curve (ice → water → steam) $\propto1/(ms)$. The lengths of the flat parts $\propto mL$.

> Ice–water mixing: first check whether the heat available can melt all the ice. If not, the final temperature is 0 °C and some ice remains. Ice at −10 °C first has to warm to 0 °C, using the specific heat of ice.

> Ice–water mixing, heating curves and "heat extracted from $x$ g of water evaporates $(1000-x)$ g" appear most years.

### Mechanical Equivalent of Heat

20. Joule's experiment: work $W$ produces heat $H$ with $W=JH$, $J=4.186$ J/cal. Falling water or a bullet stopping converts KE or PE into heat:

$$mgh=ms\Delta T\ \ (\text{or a fraction of it})$$

> Energy to heat: "a body falls through $h$ and a fraction of its PE heats it", "a bullet melts on impact".

## Heat Transfer

Heat moves by conduction (through matter, without bulk motion), convection (bulk motion of fluid) and radiation (electromagnetic waves, no medium needed).

### Thermal Conduction

21. Steady-state heat current through a rod of length $x$, area $A$, thermal conductivity $K$:

$$\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=KA\,\frac{T_1-T_2}{x}$$

$$R_{th}=\frac{x}{KA}\ \Rightarrow\ i=\frac{\Delta T}{R_{th}}$$

22. This is the same as Ohm's law: series rods $R=R_1+R_2$ (same heat current), parallel $\dfrac1R=\dfrac1{R_1}+\dfrac1{R_2}$ (same $\Delta T$). Equivalent $K$ in series (equal lengths): $\dfrac{2K_1K_2}{K_1+K_2}$. Parallel (equal areas): $\dfrac{K_1+K_2}{2}$.
23. Growth of ice on a pond (air at $-\theta$ °C, ice of thickness $x$, water at 0 °C): the time to thicken from $x_1$ to $x_2$ is

$$t=\frac{\rho L}{2K\theta}\left(x_2^2-x_1^2\right)$$

Measuring $K$: Searle's method.

> Junction temperature of series rods (equal heat current) and conduction + latent heat (ice melting rate).

### Convection

24. Natural convection: warmer, lighter fluid rises (sea breeze, land breeze, trade winds). Forced convection: a pump or fan moves the fluid. Convection needs gravity and a fluid. (Day time: breeze from sea to land.)

### Radiation, Kirchhoff, Stefan–Boltzmann

25. Prevost's theory: every body above 0 K emits radiation all the time and also absorbs it. Equilibrium means emission = absorption.
26. Emissive power $E$, absorptivity $a$. A blackbody has $a=1$ (a cavity with a small hole). Kirchhoff's law: $E/a$ is the same for all bodies at a given $T$, equal to $E_b$. Good absorbers are good emitters.

$$\frac Ea=E_b\ \Rightarrow\ e=a$$

27. Stefan–Boltzmann law (emissivity $e$, absolute temperature $T$):

$$E=e\sigma T^4,\qquad P_{net}=e\sigma A\left(T^4-T_0^4\right)$$

$$\sigma=5.67\times10^{-8}\ \text{W m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-4}$$

28. Wien's displacement law: $\lambda_mT=b=2.9\times10^{-3}$ m K. A hotter body peaks at a shorter wavelength.

> Stefan's-law ratios (doubling $T$ gives 16 times the power, area and emissivity changes) are the usual radiation questions.

### Newton's Law of Cooling

29. For a small temperature difference, the rate of cooling ∝ excess temperature:

$$-\frac{dT}{dt}=k(T-T_0)\ \Rightarrow\ T-T_0=(T_i-T_0)e^{-kt}$$

$$\text{average form: }\frac{T_1-T_2}{t}=k\left(\frac{T_1+T_2}{2}-T_0\right)$$

30. Detection of radiation: thermopile, bolometer.

> Newton's law of cooling over two intervals is repeated. Always use the excess temperature $(T-T_0)$.
